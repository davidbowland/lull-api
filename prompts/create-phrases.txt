The <context> block gives you `challengingPhraseCount`. That many of the phrases in this batch must sit at the HARDER end of recognizability; the rest are the instantly-known phrases described in the overview.

If every phrase you return is universally known, the hard puzzles have nothing to be built from and the day ships incomplete. A batch that ignores this count costs the player a puzzle.

WHAT "HARDER" MEANS -- still fair, never obscure:
- YES: a well-known novel, film or saying that a reader who knows the domain names at once and everyone else places with a hint. THE OLD MAN AND THE SEA. A STITCH IN TIME. HOIST WITH HIS OWN PETARD.
- NO: anything a reasonable adult could not place even after the third hint. The floor is "recognizable with effort", never "unknown". A separate quality pass drops these outright, so an obscure phrase is a wasted slot rather than a hard one.

<hint_rules>
Three hints, ordered from LEAST to MOST revealing. The player opens them one at a time, in order, and each one has to be worth opening.

Hints are SEMANTIC. The same phrase is reused by several puzzle types, so a hint must be about what the phrase MEANS or REFERS TO, and never about how it is written. "Starts with T" is a gift in one puzzle type, nearly useless in another, and the whole game in a third.

Every rung narrows the field. Ask at each one: a reader who knows the category and the rungs SO FAR
-- not the ones still to come -- how many phrases in this batch could they still be looking at? Name
the survivors. A count you cannot name is a count you guessed.

RUNG 1 -- narrows the category. At least one OTHER phrase in this batch must still fit. Name it.
RUNG 2 -- points from a DIFFERENT ANGLE than rung 1, and at least one other phrase must survive
          rungs 1 and 2 TOGETHER. Name it. If the answer is the only phrase left once both rungs are
          read together, rung 2 has handed it over. That is the failure this ladder exists to
          prevent and it is by far the most common one.
RUNG 3 -- near-explicit. Name the work, the speaker, or the scenario specifically enough that
          recognition is forced. Nothing else in the batch survives it.

The test is always the rungs SO FAR, read TOGETHER -- never one rung on its own. A rung 1 and a rung
2 that are individually broad can still leave exactly one phrase standing between them, and that is
a leak even though neither rung looks like one.

Each rung must leave strictly fewer phrases standing than the one before. Rung 1 must still be
strictly narrower than the category, and every rung must be true of the phrase.

RULES:
- NEVER use any word of the phrase in ANY hint or in the category. If the phrase contains STRIKES, no hint may say STRIKES.
- NEVER mention letters, spelling, first letters, word count, or how any puzzle works.
- Rung 3 must be more revealing than rung 2, and rung 2 more revealing than rung 1. A ladder whose rungs reveal the same amount wastes two of the three.
- Rungs 1 and 2 POINT AT the phrase; they do not hand it over. Rung 3 is meant to be near-explicit; rungs 1 and 2 are not.
- The three hints must differ from each other. Restating rung 1 in new words is not rung 2.
- SELF-CHECK: rungs 1 and 2 must each leave at least one OTHER phrase standing -- that is what makes
  them a ladder rather than three answers, and a rung you cannot name a survivor for is too
  revealing, not too good. A rung that leaves half the batch or more standing is too generic to be
  worth opening. Rung 3, and only rung 3, leaves nothing.
- Rung 2 must NOT be a definition, a paraphrase of the meaning, or a description of the one scene or
  line the work is famous for. A definition is an index: a reader runs it backwards and lands on the
  phrase. Point instead at the DOMAIN the phrase's image is borrowed from, the kind of situation it
  belongs to, or how it is usually used -- something another phrase in this batch could share.
- Hints and categories are prose and MAY contain digits ("a 1977 film" is a legitimate rung), even though the phrase itself may not.
